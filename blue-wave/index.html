    How the rings work

    Each ring is one beach. It is a loop of steps, and the sweep of light shows where the music is now.

    • Wave height sets how many dots light up. Bigger waves, more hits.
    • Wave period, the time between waves, sets how many steps the loop has.
    • Sea temperature sets the pitch. Warmer water, higher notes.
    • Wave direction sets where the pattern starts around the ring.
    • Random clusters bunches each ring's hits into a few tight groups, and re-rolls them every lap.
    • Drift nudges the hits along, one step at a time, so the pattern slowly changes.

    On Auto, every place gets its own key, scale, chords and sound, picked from where it is and how warm its sea is. Wave height sets how many hits, wave period sets the steps, temperature sets the pitch. The chord changes every four bars. Shift key moves everything up a tone and comes round again after six moves.

    MIDI out

    Send the rings, chord and melody to your studio. Needs Chrome or Edge.

    Every ring hit is a note. The chord is held for four bars. Four controllers follow the sea: wave energy on CC 74, wind on CC 12, wave period on CC 91, sea warmth on CC 93. Tempo goes out as MIDI clock, with start and stop, and follows the Tempo slider. In Ableton, switch on Sync for the port and press Ext.
