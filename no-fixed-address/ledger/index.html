No Fixed Address

    Sound

    Stutter makes beats roll and repeat. Grit adds crunch. Velocity chaos makes every hit a random volume, and you can see it in the dot sizes. Shuffle rolls a new set. You can also tap the middle of the rings to shuffle.

    How it works
    1. Every ring is one program or number from the news. Every dot on it is a beat. A bigger number gets more beats.
    2. Press Play. Rings join one at a time. Or press Story to hear all four chapters in order.
    3. Tap a ring, or a card in the data list, to mute it. A fact about what goes quiet takes over the screen. Mute it again and you get a different fact.
    4. A ring with only a few beats plays a short tune. Open Sound to try Glitch, Jungle or Break.
    • A big dot is a beat you can hear.
    • A tiny dot is a beat that went quiet.
    • A dashed ring is something shut down. It makes no sound.

    The four chapters tell one story: the claim, the cuts, the people and the world. The beats bunch together and spread out, and get louder and softer, a little differently on every turn. Bigger dots are harder hits. As more rings join, the earlier ones step back. Mute a ring and the others come forward.

    The facts describe what each program does. They show what is at risk, and not every cut was final. The program cuts come from CLASP, an advocacy group, using news reports from spring 2025. The savings figures come from coverage of a government audit, NPR and Musk Watch, so check the originals before you quote them. The refugee figures are from the United Nations refugee agency. They sit beside the cuts and do not claim one caused the other.
